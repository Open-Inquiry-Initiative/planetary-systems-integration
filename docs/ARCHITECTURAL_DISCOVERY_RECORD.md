# PSI-001 — Planetary Systems Integration
**Architectural discovery record | 2026-11-13 | Open Inquiry Initiative**

**Status:** Public conceptual research preview; not validated. This curated record is published independently of the private research archive.

## Abstract
A discussion beginning with the scalability of ASML-style lithography expanded, through physical constraints and energy recovery, into a hypothesis for coordinated planetary infrastructure design. The proposed framework models electricity, thermal flows, water, materials, manufacturing, transportation, ecology, human requirements and governance as coupled layers. Its claim is not that a globally optimized design exists or has been demonstrated; it is that multi-layer coordination can be compared experimentally with independently optimized systems.

## Discovery sequence (chronological reconstruction)
1. **Precision fabrication:** Asked what scaling lithography from a facility to planetary dimensions would mean. Distinguished throughput scaling from maintaining nanometer registration over huge distances.
2. **Environmental constraints:** Considered vibration, expansion, radiation, gravity, contamination and waste heat. Orbital vacuum eliminates convection; heat can still escape by radiation. Lunar gravity is about one-sixth Earth's, not one-third.
3. **Thermal-first architecture:** Proposed designing manufacturing around heat transport and rejection rather than bolting cooling onto machinery.
4. **Distributed thermal networks:** Generalized to buildings, industry, computing and transportation; local heat reuse and district networks are preferable to assuming a single planetary heat pipe.
5. **Recursive heat utilization:** Proposed cascading thermal energy across uses at progressively lower temperatures. Exergy, rather than total energy alone, bounds useful work. Recursion stops when marginal recoverable benefit no longer exceeds costs and losses.
6. **Planetary redesign:** Imagined a hypothetical 'God mode' redesign of Earth's infrastructure using 2026 technologies, subject to physical laws.
7. **Layer overlays:** Combined thermal networks, smart grids, water, manufacturing, transport, ecosystems and human systems on a geographic model.
8. **Systems-of-systems insight:** Local subsystem optimization can conflict with global outcomes; overly centralized integration can also amplify cascading failures.
9. **Reproducibility:** Proposed layer-by-layer requirements, interfaces, assumptions, scenarios, stress tests, revisions and provenance.

## Candidate architecture
- L00 Physical constraints: conservation laws, thermodynamics, geography, environmental boundaries.
- L01 Energy: generation, conversion, electricity networks, storage.
- L02 Thermal/exergy: heat quality, capture, transfer, heat pumps, cascades, storage and rejection.
- L03 Water: supply, treatment, cooling demands, watershed constraints.
- L04 Materials/manufacturing: extraction, precision fabrication, recycling, logistics.
- L05 Built environment: settlements, transport, communications and industry.
- L06 Human/ecological systems: safety, health, access, land and biodiversity.
- L07 Coordination: interoperable models, decentralized controls, feedback, fault isolation.
- L08 Verification: baselines, sensitivity analyses, adversarial tests and independent replication.

## Preliminary illustrative calculations (NOT empirical findings)
- Reference world annual primary energy: **650 EJ/year**, a rounded illustrative input, not a validated 2026 measurement.
- Hypothetical 9% reduction: **58.5 EJ/year**, or approximately **1.85 TW** averaged over a year. This is arithmetic on an assumed saving rate, not a predicted benefit.
- Candidate reference region: 100,000 people, 100 km², 500 GWh/year electricity demand and 300 GWh/year useful heat demand. These are invented simulation inputs.
- Simplified recovery model: E_saved = E_baseline × f_recoverable × f_collected × f_useful. It omits temperature matching, heat-pump work, temporal/geographic mismatch and lifecycle costs; it must not be interpreted as proof of achievable savings.
- Carnot limit: η_max = 1 − T_c/T_h (temperatures in kelvin), under idealized heat-engine assumptions.
- Thermal radiation: P = εσA(T⁴ − T_environment⁴), for a simplified radiative exchange geometry.

## Hypothesis
**H-PSI-001:** Under specified physical, economic, environmental and human-service constraints, coordinated multi-layer infrastructure planning can outperform independently optimized planning on at least some Pareto-efficient combinations of energy consumption, resilience, material use and service quality.

## Minimum reproducible experiment
1. Fix region boundary, time horizon, weather, population, industry and service demand.
2. Establish identical data and constraints for two models: A, subsystem-local optimization; B, coordinated multi-layer optimization.
3. Track electricity, useful heat, source temperatures, heat-pump work, storage state, water use, capital/operating costs, emissions, service quality and outage response.
4. Require conservation-of-energy checks, exergy accounting, capacity and temperature constraints, and temporal matching.
5. Test failures: heat-network outage, grid islanding, drought, heat wave, cold snap, equipment failure and bad sensor data.
6. Publish model code, input datasets or synthetic substitutes, configuration, seeds, results and known limitations.
7. Compare distributions and Pareto frontiers; report negative findings and sensitivity to assumptions.

## Open questions
- Which layer interactions provide measurable benefits, and which introduce unacceptable coupling?
- How do regional climates and heat-demand timing change results?
- When do distributed networks beat centralized ones?
- How much benefit is attributable to relocation versus retrofit?
- What governance prevents an optimizer from sacrificing human welfare for a narrow efficiency score?
